# Municipality of Collesano — DIRECTIVES FOR THE FORMATION OF THE GENERAL URBAN PLAN

## Introduction

Regional Law n. 19/2020 introduced, as regards the level of municipal planning, new instruments that differ significantly, both in content and in formation procedures, from the instruments that the Municipalities have used until now. In particular, the instrument of the Piano Regolatore Generale (PRG) [General Regulatory Plan] has been replaced by the Piano Urbanistico Generale (PUG) [General Urban Plan], and the implementing planning instruments, which until now had various names (detailed plan, subdivision plan, recovery plan,…..), have been replaced by instruments with the single name of Piani Particolareggiati Attuativi (PUA) [Detailed Implementing Plans], albeit with differentiated contents in relation to the cases to which they refer. The PUG is a mandatory instrument for all Municipalities and, once approved, definitively replaces the current General Regulatory Plan.

## 1. Legal nature and technical contents of the PUG

The PUG instrument appears, both in its contents and in its procedures, significantly different from the PRG. As regards contents, the substantial difference concerns the function of the plan, which from an instrument regulating the rents connected with the building use of land becomes a project for the regeneration of the built environment, in the various forms it takes in the territory, and for the conservation and restoration of the landscape.

The most significant difference, as regards procedures, concerns instead the structure of the PUG in two distinct phases; the first phase leads to the approval of a Preliminary Document of the PUG, the second to the approval of the definitive PUG project. Unlike the previous legislation on PRGs, which also provided for the approval of an outline Scheme of the PRG but without any legal effect, the preliminary Document of the PUG instead produces well-identified legal effects that may also concern extensive parts of the municipal territory.

That said, it is observed that law 19/2020 places the PUG within a complex system of other plans, the Regional Territorial Plan and the Consortium Territorial Plan, and subordinates its approval to the approval of the latter. The obligation for municipalities to approve the PUG is triggered in particular one year after the approval of the PTR. And indeed the PUG appears, reading the provisions of the law, strongly conditioned by both the PTR and the PTC; in them, first the Region and then the Consortia of municipalities must set the directives for the drafting of the municipal plans. This does not prevent, however, by virtue of a principle of flexibility, but also by explicit legal provision, the PUG from being not only started but also from acquiring legal validity without the PTC having yet been approved. In art. 26, in paragraph 7 which refers to the approval of the Preliminary plan, a double regime is in fact established for the case in which the PTR and PTC have already been approved and another for the case in which they have not yet been approved. It is therefore clearly deduced that at least the preliminary Document of the PUG can be approved before the approval of the higher-level plans, albeit with a particular procedure.

The **first step** for the drafting of the PUG is the approval, by the Municipal Executive of the lead municipality, of a **direction act** with which the directives concerning the formation of the Plan are issued. The law does not further specify the content of this direction act, but there is reason to believe, from reading the law but also from the combined provisions of the statutory rules governing the Executive's powers, that this administrative act is entirely different from the "*General directives for the formation of the Plan*", with which, under the previous urban-planning rules, the PRG process was started. Firstly, because the Executive has, in the regional system, taken up in the Municipal Statute, predominantly executive and organisational rather than programmatic tasks, but also for another more complex reason to be found in the relationship the new law establishes between the PUG and the higher-level plans. It is in fact the Region's task, through the PTR, to give the municipalities directions for the design of the PUG (art. 19, paragraph 6 point c) and the municipal Consortia's task to define the dimensioning of the plan and the design criteria to be adopted (art. 22, paragraph 2, point g). This is the reason that led the legislator to eliminate the passage through the Council of the Directives for the formation of the Plan, which was instead provided for PRGs; the Directives have in fact been moved from the municipal to the supra-municipal level. A de facto subordination is thus established between the PUG and these instruments.

Returning to the contents of the direction Act, it is therefore considered that it should concern above all the operational methods through which the PUG formation process must develop, concerning firstly the opportunity to proceed with the formation of the plan in associated form with other municipalities for more effective territorial coordination, the criteria for selecting the Plan's designers and the technicians drafting the sector studies, the criteria for guaranteeing citizens' participation in the plan formation process, but also, where the Region and the Consortia have not defined them, the general criteria, above all of a methodological nature, to be followed in the design.

The second step in the PUG formation process is the identification and appointment of the Procedure Manager. This figure takes on a very important role in the plan formation process, since the law entrusts him/her with administrative tasks of great responsibility, namely:

1) publishes on the official notice board and on the municipality's *web* site a notice of the start of the PUG formation procedure, specifying the ways in which interested parties can put forward proposals and formulate suggestions
2) identifies the ways in which to consult and involve, in the subsequent phases of the procedure, public and private parties and the representatives of the professional orders and colleges who, by their specific competences and responsibilities, are interested in the plan, possibly also through the constitution of a *forum* for consultations
3) transmits to the Municipal Council the preliminary Document of the PUG for adoption and subsequently the PUG project
4) calls the planning conferences
5) orders the publication of the Preliminary plan and subsequently of the PUG.

The obligations subsequent to those described so far, which in reality can however be adopted at the same time as the others described so far (the law specifies nothing about the temporal sequence of these obligations), concern the identification and **appointment of the designer and of the drafters of the sector studies**.

As regards the **design of the PUG**, the new law (art. 26) provides that it may be "*entrusted, where necessary, to professionals engaged for the purpose and consultants who are qualified in urban-territorial planning, including through recourse to design competitions*". This indication, contained in art. 26, must be connected with the others, contained in arts. 13 and 14, which provide that each municipality equip itself with a technical structure for the planning of its own territory and for the management of the Territorial Information System. The constitution of a specific **municipal Plan Office** therefore appears necessary, within which to identify the Design Manager, who must necessarily be a master's/specialist graduate qualified for the profession; this office may, as mentioned, also avail itself of external technicians and consultants provided they are qualified in urban-territorial planning. In case of unavailability, but also at the Administration's discretion, as an alternative to the constitution of the municipal Design Office, an assignment may be conferred on one or more external professionals provided they have proven experience. The new law in fact no longer establishes a priority of the internal assignment over a professional assignment external to the Authority, referring however to a condition of necessity.

As regards the **sector studies**, the new law (art. 26) provides that they consist of the following (*in brackets the Decree of the Regional Minister for Territory and Environment defining their contents*):

1) **Agricultural-forestry study (SAF) (D.A. 119 of 16/07/2021)**. Under the previous legislation, this study was fundamentally aimed at identifying areas with specialised crops to be excluded from building use and at classifying and delimiting woods, forest belts and riparian formations, to be subjected to legal constraints. The new law considerably expands the tasks assigned to this study, since it introduces a new definition (art. 37) of rural territory whose classification and zoning can only be based on a specific specialist study.
2) **Geological study (D.A. 120 of 16/07/2021)**. Compared with the previous legislation, which remains substantially confirmed, only the clarification is added that this study must contain a particular reference to "*hydrogeological aspects (P.A.I.)*".
3) **Hydraulic compatibility study (D.A. 117 of 17/07/2021)**. Its task is to: "*a. verify the urban-planning provisions in relation to the level of hazard; b. define the admissible urban-planning provisions and the activities that may be carried out; c. identify the rules of use and the implementing prescriptions; d. identify the interventions suitable to guarantee the hydraulic structure of the territory and compliance with the principle of hydraulic invariance; e. identify the construction indications for the application of vulnerability-reduction techniques (floodproofing)*". It should be noted that some of these services, notably those under letter d), could go beyond the traditional competences of the geologist, crossing into the field of hydraulic engineering. It is therefore necessary to evaluate the possible opportunity of extending the assignment already given for the geological study, or, in relation to the characteristics of the municipal territory and its criticality from a hydrogeological point of view, of giving a new assignment to a hydraulic engineer specialised in the sector.
4) **Demographic and socio-economic study (D.A. 144 of 29/09/2021)**. The new law assigns to this study, which has traditionally been part of the analyses needed for drafting the PRG, an autonomous substance and a preparatory character for the PUG. It is therefore necessary to proceed, for this study too, with the identification of the person who must draft it.
5) **Archaeological study.** The law prescribes that this study must be drafted by the competent Superintendency for Cultural and Environmental Heritage, at the Municipality's request. The municipal Administration must therefore request its drafting.
6) **SEA Environmental Report (D.A. 271 of 23/12/2021 and D.A. n. 167 of 12/05/2023).** On this matter, the current regulatory provisions, already long applied to PRGs, remain substantially valid.

On the basis of the studies described above, and taking account of the Executive's direction act and the observations received in the (thirty-day) publication period of the notice of the start of the procedure, the Plan Office must, within ninety days of the start of the procedure, draw up the **preliminary Document of the PUG**, accompanied by the **preliminary Environmental Report of the SEA**. Before forwarding the PUG preliminary to the Municipal Council for approval, the Procedure Manager must call (in the absence of the PTR) a specific **planning Conference** aimed at verifying the coherence of the preliminary document's indications with the general and wide-area frameworks and objectives, inviting all the **competent public bodies** to take part. Subsequently, within sixty days of forwarding the outcome of the conference, the Plan must be discussed and approved by the Municipal Council. The adopted preliminary Plan is published in full on the websites and for information in the GURS. In the following ninety days the **definitive PUG** must be drawn up, according to the Guidelines issued by specific Decree (**D.A. 116 of 17/07/2021**) of the Regional Minister for Territory and Environment. It should be noted that the adoption of the Preliminary plan entails the application of the safeguard measures.

## 2. Methodological directives for drafting the PUG

For the reasons expressed in the introduction, it is considered that the Directives for drafting the PUG, unlike the Directives that referred to PRGs, should be limited to formulating strategic directions and indications of a methodological nature. Such an approach, besides responding to the legislative wording, allows the floor to be left, as regards decisions specifically concerning local urban-planning issues, to those most directly concerned, namely the citizens and the associations that represent them in various ways, who as mentioned in the introduction will be involved in a process of participation in decisions that constitutes one of the most qualifying points of the new urban-planning law.

The Directives Document will therefore be supplemented with the indications that will be given by the institutional bodies and by the representatives of diffuse interests in the publication phase of the same resolution. The designer must refer to these Directives, thus supplemented, for the preparation of the preliminary Document of the PUG.

Below is therefore given, first, a very brief examination of the legal situation to be taken into account in drafting the PUG, and then some indications concerning the principles by which the new planning must be inspired.

## 3. The legal situation

### 3.1 The framework of constraints.

In drafting the PUG, numerous constraints acting on the municipal territory must be taken into consideration, which can broadly be identified as follows:

#### A) Constraints deriving from laws

- River constraint under arts. 93 et seq. of R.D. n. 523 of 25.07.1904. The strips of absolute non-buildability of 10 m from the foot of the embankments, banks and defences of all watercourses (rivers, streams, natural incisions, canals and ditches in which public waters flow, even seasonally) must be considered (refer to the cadastral map).
- Constraints under art. 15 of Regional Law 78/1976 concerning the coastal strip.
- Constraints deriving from art. 10 of Regional Law 16/1996, as amended, relating to woods.
- Constraints deriving from the presence of wells for drinking water pursuant to art. 94 of D.L. 152/2006. The zones of absolute protection (not less than 10 m), of respect (equal to 200 metres radius from the point of abstraction or derivation, unless otherwise regulated by the Regions) and any other protection zones (identified by the Regions within the catchment basins and aquifer recharge areas) must be considered, specifying for each the relative regulatory regime.
- Constraints deriving from the presence of road infrastructure, deriving from the Highway Code (art. 28 D.P.R. 495/1992 as amended). For each infrastructure, the relative setback strip must be considered, distinguishing whether the infrastructure is inside or outside built-up areas.
- Power-line constraint. At the sides of existing external overhead lines, in order to limit exposure to electric and magnetic fields in residential settlements, in implementation of Framework Law 36/2001 (Art. 4, paragraph 1, point h) as amended, a setback strip must be provided for buildings used as dwellings or in which activities involving prolonged stays are in any case carried out, of a width equal to that established, for each nominal voltage, by the Ministerial Decree of 29/05/2008.
- Aqueduct and oil-pipeline constraint. At the sides of the pressurised pipelines crossing the municipal territory, a protection strip of a minimum width of 1,50 m on each side of the pipeline axis is established.
- Cemetery respect strips pursuant to the Consolidated Health Laws and art. 28 of Law 166/2002. A respect strip around the perimeter wall of existing cemeteries must be considered as determined by municipal measures or, in their absence, of 200 m.
- Respect strip for purification plants pursuant to art. 46 of Regional Law 27/1986. The strips of absolute non-buildability established on the basis of the characteristics of the purification plant must be considered
- Constraint on areas swept by fire, pursuant to art. 10 of Law n. 353 of 21 November 2000 as amended. The wooded areas and pastures whose surfaces have been swept by fire must be considered, distinguished by year, in which building is prohibited for ten years (from the event) and which may not have a use different from that pre-existing the fire for at least fifteen years.
- Respect constraint for natural-gas transport and distribution plants (Ministerial Decree 17 April 2008). The respect strips as determined in the reference rule must be considered, specifying for each area the limitations imposed.
- Civic Uses constraint under art. 26 of regional law 27 April 1999, n. 10. The perimeters of the areas subject to civic Uses must be considered, specifying the relative constraint regime.
- State cattle-track (trazzera) domain constraint (art. 25 of Regional Law n. 10 of 27/04/1999 as amended). The areas falling within the trazzera Domain must be considered, specifying the relative limitations.

#### B) Constraints deriving from the provisions of general and sectoral territorial plans

- Regional Territorial Plan (art. 19 of Regional Law 19/2020) - Any constraint prescriptions introduced by the plan (when it is drafted) must be considered, specifying their regulatory regimes. At present, the Guidelines of the Plan were approved by Resolution of the Government Executive of 17 June 2021, which constitute a reference to be taken into consideration in the design of the preliminary Document of the PUG:
- Regional Landscape Territorial Plan (P.T.P.R.) and Area Plans of the Landscape Territorial Plan. The perimeters of the areas as identified in the map of regulatory regimes of the Area plans must be considered, specifying for each of them (level 1, 2 and 3 areas and recovery areas) the limitations and constraints imposed on building activity. It is emphasised that, since the area plan of the province of Palermo has not yet been adopted, it is appropriate (but not obligatory) to take account of the draft plan already transmitted to the Municipalities by the Superintendency for sharing.
- Basin excerpt Plans for the Hydrogeological Structure. The perimeters of the areas of geomorphological and hydraulic hazard and of the attention sites as identified in the excerpt Plans relating to the basins affecting the municipal territory must be considered, specifying for each area the relative constraint regime.
- Regional plan for quarry materials and valuable stone materials (Quarry plan). The perimeters of the areas possibly affected by the plan must be considered, specifying for each of them the relative regulatory regimes.
- Integrated Regional Project of the Sicilian Ecological Network. The perimeters of the areas affected by the Plan must be considered, specifying for each of them the relative regulatory regimes.
- Sicilian Regional Environmental Energy Plan (PEARS). The perimeters of the areas possibly affected by the plan must be considered, specifying for each of them the relative regulatory regimes.
- Regional flood-risk management Plan. The perimeters of the areas subject to flood risk as identified in the plan must be considered, specifying for each area the relative constraint regime.
- Asbestos-risk plan pursuant to Regional Law 10 of 29/06/2014. The perimeters of the risk areas as identified in the documents drafted pursuant to art. 3 lett. c) of law 10/2014 must be considered, specifying for each area the limitations imposed.

#### C) Constraints deriving from administrative acts.

- Hydrogeological constraint under R.D. 3267/1923. The areas subjected to the constraint by specific administrative acts, which subordinate building activity to obtaining an opinion of the District Forestry Inspectorate, must be considered.
- Geosite constraint imposed pursuant to Regional Law 11 April 2012, n. 25. The perimeters of any areas identified in specific implementing Decrees must be considered, specifying for each area the limitations imposed.
- Public-interest constraints imposed pursuant to the Cultural Heritage Code, arts. 10 et seq. of D.Lgs n. 42/2004. The perimeters of the areas subject to protection must be considered, specifying the relative constraint regimes as specified in the specific administrative acts.

To these indications must be added those contained in the Guidelines for drafting the PUG, issued by the regional Department of territory and environment (D.A. n. 116 of 06/07/2021), which, with reference to the PUG formation process, add that in the PUG, and in particular in a specific document called the "map of constraints", the constraints deriving from the sector studies preparatory to the PUG must also be represented, distinguishing the level of modifiability that their presence and overlap determines in the various parts of the municipal territory.

## 4. General design directions

### 4.1 The structural invariants

All the limitations and constraints described in the previous paragraph must be considered invariant elements in the design of the new Plan. Likewise, any constraint indications deriving from the preparatory sector studies — geological, agricultural-forestry, hydraulic compatibility, archaeological — must be considered invariant elements in the design of the plan.

In the first, any areas subject to instability of a geological or hydraulic nature or affected by tectonic formations or by seismic risk in general must be indicated and delimited, in which new buildings may not be permitted, as well as the areas in which new buildings may be executed observing particular precautions. These indications must concern in particular the areas of new urbanisation provided for in the plan; a verification of a geological and hydraulic nature must in any case be carried out, for the purposes of their possible reconfirmation in the new plan, also for the areas already affected by urbanisation provisions of the current Plans. The new geological study must be drafted in compliance with the regulatory provisions in force in the Sicilian Region, referring in particular to the operational indications contained in D. Dir. n. 120 of 14/07/2021 and, for hydraulic compatibility, in D. Dir. n. 117 of 07/07/2021.

The hydraulic compatibility study must show the perimeters of the areas subject to limitations, distinguishing them on the basis of the classification proposed in the same Study. In the agricultural-forestry study, besides indicating and exactly delimiting the wooded areas and the areas of scrub and riparian vegetation pursuant to art. 10 of Regional Law 16/1996 as amended and of the Regional Presidential Decree of June 2000, for the purposes of applying the constraint mentioned above, the strategic agricultural areas must also be identified. For these areas the plan may not provide, except in cases of proven and unavoidable necessity, uses other than agricultural ones. All possible indications concerning the protection of certain areas of historical and documentary interest must also be considered. Finally, for each of the areas of archaeological interest identified in the archaeological study produced by the Superintendency, the type of constraint or protection to be adopted and any consequent limitations on building activity must be defined.

### 4.2 The plan-formation procedure and the strategic environmental assessment

The new plan, like all urban and territorial planning instruments, must be subjected to the SEA procedure, in implementation of what is established by Legislative Decree n. 152/2006 as amended, in order to minimise or mitigate the environmental impact of the works provided for in it. Within the preliminary SEA report, the need to subject the plan's provisions to the VINCA [habitats impact] verification must also be checked, in relation to the presence, outside the municipal territory but close to it, of areas belonging to the Natura 2000 Network.

### 4.3 Relations with current planning

In designing the plan, it is necessary to take account of the fact that its provisions will affect a territory whose legal situation has been precisely defined by the previous PRG and by a set, previously described, of supra-local planning instruments. This observation takes on greater relevance in relation to what is provided for by the Decree-law of May 2001 regulating the expropriation procedure for public utility; it provides in fact that expropriation constraints, lapsed through the elapsed period of validity, if re-proposed in the new urban-planning instruments, must be compensated. This provision, which came into force from January 2002, ties the implementation of the new Plan to the availability of the sums needed for the compensations.

In drafting the new plan, therefore, the opportunity of reconfirming the expropriation constraints must be evaluated case by case with the utmost attention, which in principle should in any case be admitted only in cases of proven necessity. Conversely, the non-binding provisions of the current PRG may be confirmed if they do not conflict with the system of structural invariants, with the protection principles underlying the plan, or with regulatory provisions introduced after the approval of the Plan. As regards the technical-regulatory content of the Plan, the need is emphasised, as already mentioned, to limit as much as possible the provision of constraints preordained to expropriation, given that they lapse just five years after the approval of the Plan, deferring their provision to executive plans of public initiative or, more appropriately, of private initiative. If to this is added the fact that with judgment 348/07 of the Constitutional Court and with the subsequent ratification by the 2008 budget law, the compensation in expropriation for public utility was almost doubled, measuring it against the market value of the asset to be expropriated, one easily realises how profoundly the role of the urban plan has changed and how unproductive the consolidated practice of imposing, through the PRG, expropriation constraints can now be considered.

### 4.4 Relations with regional and consortium/metropolitan planning

As stated in the introduction, the law establishes a precise subordination of the PUG to the regional-level Territorial Plan and to the Consortium Plan, introduced by the same law. In reality, however, these plans have not yet been drafted. To date, as already mentioned, only a Direction Act for the Regional Territorial Plan has been drafted, approved by Resolution of the Government Executive n. 263 of 17 June 2021. It sets the mandatory principles on which the governing action of the Region and of the local Authorities must be based. These principles, set out in the following chapter, must be adopted as guidance lines for the design of the PUG.

## 5. Strategic directions

Consistently with what is said in the introduction, there follow, rather than precise intervention indications concerning the themes of local planning, the general principles to which the new Plan must adhere. From compliance with these principles will derive the strategic lines and the operational provisions of the plan, which must be defined through dialogue with public and private parties, bearers of collective interests. The function of territorial government must be performed in compliance with the principles of integration, efficiency, economy, equity and impartiality of administrative action, as well as in compliance with the following principles of effectiveness of territorial-government action, set by the legislator in the first articles of Regional Law 19/2020:

**Sustainability and quality**. Planning must actively contribute to containing land consumption as a common good and non-renewable resource that performs functions and produces ecosystem services and that must be protected also for the prevention and mitigation of hydrogeological instability events. The policies of protection and enhancement of the landscape, of containment of land consumption and of sustainable territorial development must be coordinated with territorial and landscape planning and pursue the protection and enhancement of the agricultural function through the reduction of land consumption and the agroforestry use of abandoned agricultural land, favouring interventions of reuse and recovery of degraded urbanised areas

**Reuse and urban regeneration**. In drafting the plan, priority must be given to the census of the areas of the municipal territory already affected by building processes but unused or susceptible to regeneration, recovery, redevelopment; and they must also proceed, within the censused areas, to the constitution and keeping of a list of the areas susceptible to priority use for building purposes of urban regeneration and for the location of new productive and infrastructural investments. Interventions for the recovery of historical areas or disused urban areas, as well as for the recovery of rural settlements through maintenance, renovation, restoration, conservative rehabilitation of existing buildings and of rural roads and environmental conservation of the territory, must be favoured through adequate incentivising urban-planning regulation.

**Equalisation and compensation**. The provisions contained in the plan must be implemented according to equalisation-compensation criteria and instruments, in order to ensure greater efficiency and justice in public policies and in the planning function, as well as a fair and extensive distribution of advantages and burdens among the owners of the areas affected by urban transformation, sterilising parasitic rent and incentivising entrepreneurial profitability. Urban equalisation must be achieved through the fairest and most effective distribution of building rights and burdens among the owners of areas included in ambits, even discontinuous, identified by the plan, and may also extend to the entire municipal territory with the appropriate regulations. Equalisation must also be achieved through urban, landscape and environmental compensation that facilitates the enhancement of resources.

**Concertation and participation**. Last but not least, the principle must be established that all the provisions of the PUG must be filtered through a process of involvement of territorial actors, of bodies but also of citizens in the decision-making choices. Participation, as moreover prescribed in Law 19/2020 and in the Guidelines for drafting the PUG, must constitute the method to be followed in designing the new structure of the territory.

In compliance with these principles, the new plan must pursue the following strategic directions:

1) ensure that urban and territorial transformation processes are compatible with safety and the reduction of territorial risks, the health and quality of life of citizens, preserve the physical features of the territory from irreversible alterations and maintain its historical-cultural identity with adequate actions for the recovery of degraded or compromised sites, in compliance with the provisions on the protection of cultural heritage and the other sector regulations affecting urban-building activity;
2) promote the enhancement and improvement of the environmental, architectural, cultural and social qualities of the town and territory, through interventions to redevelop the existing fabric, aimed also at eliminating territorial imbalances;
3) enhance the urban historic centre and the other built areas of historical and environmental interest, as well as the complexes of scattered houses, rural settlements and individual buildings of historical interest, promoting and implementing interventions of urban restoration, urban redevelopment, renovation and building recovery, through immediately applicable regulation (minimising deferral to executive planning) allowing an urban transformation compatible with their historical identity but oriented to their correct insertion in the current context. For this purpose, reference may be made to the Study of the historic centre drafted in application of Regional Law 13/2015 and already operative, adopting it as a starting point for the drafting of the implementing planning necessary to give legal completeness to the enhancement initiatives;
4) pursue the reduction of land consumption, limiting it to cases in which no valid alternatives exist but always in a logic of compactness and containment of urbanised areas. Provisions for transformation or new-construction interventions, where deemed necessary, must in any case be reconciled with the needs of safeguarding the available natural, landscape-environmental, agro-silvo-pastoral and historical-cultural resources;
5) determine the settlement needs to which the new plan must respond in a logic of containment, taking account of the possibilities of recovery and reuse of the existing building stock;
6) design the infrastructural and services system taking account of the real possibilities of realisation, taking account of the functional characteristics of the centre and of its functional specialisation in the territorial context;

The objectives set out so far cannot in any case be achieved except through broad sharing by the community, which must have a fundamental role during the entire process of construction of the Plan.

## 6. Directives and strategy of the Plan

From a strategic point of view, for effective urban planning, the actions to be undertaken must coincide with a policy of urban recovery and redevelopment through:

1. Planning of an organic structure of the territory to foster the development of the community and the economy;
2. Enhancement of the cultural and landscape assets present in the territory;
3. Fostering the development of economic activities;
4. Improving the usability of the territory from a tourism point of view.
