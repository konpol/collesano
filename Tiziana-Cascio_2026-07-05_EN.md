# English Version

**Tiziana Cascio**
5.07.2026
<https://www.facebook.com/share/v/1Jea53rzvd/>

## Overall summary

The speech is a detailed public rebuttal. Tiziana Cascio says she and her administration originally wanted to hold a normal public meeting about what they had done in three years, what projects were ongoing, and what problems they had faced. Instead, she says they were forced to respond to public accusations from former mayor Giovanni Meli.

Her main message is: the administration accepts criticism, but rejects claims that it is incompetent, anti-democratic, negligent, or inactive. She repeatedly says she is speaking "with documents," meaning she is basing her reply on official papers, dates, messages, resolutions, funding requests, and administrative acts.

## Main translated points

### 1. Response to personal and political accusations

Cascio says Meli described her administration as extremely poor, unworthy of governing, childish, irresponsible, and not really political. She says she does not want to play the victim, but she rejects the tone and substance of those attacks.

She especially objects to the claim that people in Collesano are afraid to speak, afraid of retaliation, or living under an undemocratic administration. She says that describing her as less democratic than Putin is not normal political criticism, but an insult to her role as mayor and to the whole community of Collesano.

She asks the audience directly whether they came freely and whether anyone has actually suffered retaliation. Her point is that, if such things happened, they should be reported formally, not used as slogans in a public rally.

### 2. "We did not betray anyone"

A major theme is her relationship with Giovanni Meli. Cascio says she had supported him in the past, served as president of the council, and was part of his administration. She rejects his story of betrayal, saying that after she became mayor there were still messages and contacts between them, including advice and reminders about administrative issues.

She argues that many projects Meli now describes as "his" were actually carried out by a team that included people now in Cascio's administration. She repeatedly says: he was not mayor alone, just as she is not mayor alone.

Her criticism is that Meli speaks too much in terms of "I did this," "I financed that," "my project," whereas the previous administration's results belonged to the whole team.

## Key administrative topics

### 3. Urban planning: PRG and PUG

Cascio explains that Collesano already has a town planning instrument, the PRG, from 2008. She acknowledges that it is old and needs updating.

She says the update process began under the Meli administration in 2012 and reached approval of a preliminary framework in May 2023. However, the updated PRG was not finally approved by the deadline of 31 December 2025.

She accepts political responsibility for that missed deadline. But she rejects the idea that the administration did nothing or tried to blame others. She says they immediately began the process for the new planning instrument, the PUG, including:

- a municipal executive resolution giving direction for the PUG;
- creation of the municipal urban planning office;
- appointment of the procedure manager;
- public notices and citizen observations;
- meetings with professionals;
- a request for regional funding to avoid burdening the municipal budget.

Her message: yes, the PRG update was not completed on time, but the administration took responsibility and moved immediately toward the PUG.

### 4. Casa di Comunità / community health services

This is one of the most important parts of the speech.

Cascio says she never denied that Collesano had been included in a regional health plan for a Casa di Comunità spoke, meaning a local community health facility. She says what she denied was different: there was no direct funding assigned to the Municipality of Collesano.

According to her explanation, the regional plan existed, but implementation belonged to the health authority, not directly to the municipality. She says the amount involved was around €342,000, which she suggests would not have been enough to adapt the former small hospital building.

She reads from documents saying the intervention was cancelled because it was technically or economically impracticable, especially due to structural and seismic issues, not because of negligence by the municipal administration.

She then explains that the municipality proposed alternative municipal buildings, including the former slaughterhouse and the Opera Pia building. Eventually, the administration approved a free-use agreement with ASP Palermo for municipal premises to be used for health services for 35 years.

The intended services include:

- specialist outpatient clinics;
- community services;
- PUA, the "single access point" for services;
- territorial health services;
- continuity of care.

A health official, Dr. Casiglia, also speaks. He confirms that the original former hospital building could not realistically be restored with the available funding. He explains the difference between hub and spoke health structures and says Collesano should still receive essential services similar to those planned for Pollina, including diabetology, cardiology, pulmonology, geriatrics, nursing services, booking/access services, home care access, and telemedicine.

The key translated conclusion: Collesano may not get the original Casa di Comunità building as planned, but the administration says it secured equivalent essential health services through another arrangement.

### 5. Opera Pia and social services

Cascio says there has been confusion between several different projects connected to the Opera Pia building.

She separates them into different items:

- First, the ATS social infrastructure project, which began around 2021–2022 and is already active. It involves services for around 200 citizens, including elderly people, disabled people, fragile people, socialization activities, home assistance, and support for children with special needs.
- Second, another project promoted through the social-health district to strengthen services for minors and families.
- Third, a project connected to disabled children and family support.

She argues that Meli mixed these projects together and presented them inaccurately.

### 6. Wastewater treatment plant / depuratore

Another large part concerns the depuratore, the wastewater treatment plant.

Cascio says Collesano's treatment plant is old, dating from 1975, but it is legal, functioning, and not subject to EU infringement proceedings. She emphasizes this because she says the opposition made it sound like an emergency or legal failure.

She explains that under the previous administration, in 2021, a design assignment was given for upgrading and improving the plant. She acknowledges that the project existed, but says it was not ready to be built because it still needed several opinions and authorizations, including from ATO, the regional energy/public utilities department, ASP, the heritage authority, and civil engineering authorities.

She says a services conference was opened but then closed because issues remained, including urban planning conformity and other technical questions. Her administration then worked to resolve those issues, including meetings with ATI and technical offices. She says the services conference was finally concluded positively in March 2024.

After that, the project still needed to be updated to the newer regional price list and the new procurement code. She says they settled a dispute with the designer, obtained an updated project, had it technically verified, approved it administratively, and submitted it for a funding line.

Her central rebuttal is: there was no lost financing decree for the Collesano depuratore. She says there had been a call for project sheets or proposals, but not an actual secured funding decree. She tries to support this by playing audio from a technical official who, according to her, confirms there was no actual financing already assigned.

### 7. The legal dispute with the designer

Cascio explains that the designer of the wastewater plant project requested an additional payment of about €28,000 beyond the original fee. The municipal offices decided to protect the authority by appointing a lawyer. She says this was not scandalous, but normal caution.

Eventually, the municipality settled the case for a lower amount, around €19,000, and immediately received the updated project needed to apply for funding.

Her argument: the settlement was practical and useful for the municipality, because it avoided litigation risk and allowed the project to move forward.

## Projects the administration claims as its own

A big portion of the speech is a long list of projects Cascio says were either started, continued, funded, completed, or prepared by her administration.

She stresses the difference between a real funded project and a project merely placed in a ranking or submitted for funding. She apologizes for one campaign statement where she had described a sports facility project as funded when it had actually only been admitted to a ranking but not financed.

Major projects she mentions include:

### Asilo nido / nursery school

A PNRR-funded project from 2024 to convert existing premises into a municipal nursery. She says the project received around €560,000, with works delivered in 2025 and completed in June 2026. The administration is now moving toward opening the nursery.

### Road works

She lists several road-related interventions, including:

- €80,000 for urban and rural road improvements;
- works in Contrada Croce and Contrada Burgifuto;
- €800,000 FSC funds for roads in the town and districts such as Comune, Arivuzza, San Basilio, Favara;
- about €150,000 from ESA for the Blanci-Gennaro road;
- a further planned continuation of that road project;
- another project of around €140,000 for town roads;
- €30,000 for road design work.

### Case popolari / public housing area

She describes a technical-economic feasibility project for the public housing area, including parking, green areas, sidewalks, benches, and external improvements.

### Contrada Scaletta

A project prepared with help from GAL Madonie for redeveloping the Contrada Scaletta area.

### Municipal building energy efficiency

A project of about €1.5 million for improving the municipal building, including windows, heating systems, boilers, and energy efficiency.

### Sports facilities

She mentions:

- about €60,000 used to reopen sports fields;
- a project for a covered multipurpose basketball/sports facility near the middle school, with approvals from the heritage authority and CONI.

### Water network and drought response

She says the administration worked on the water network through PNRR-related projects and emergency measures. These include:

- replacement of meters;
- replacement of sections of water network in several areas;
- dividing a large €6 million water network project into three functional lots to make it more fundable;
- applying for funding for the first lot;
- around €160,000 from civil protection for the Pigno well;
- a new water tanker;
- smart valves to manage opening and closing of wells remotely;
- interventions recovering about five hours of water per day;
- a system to manage public fountains more efficiently and reduce waste.

### Waste collection center

She discusses the CCR, the municipal collection center, described as a PNRR project from the previous administration that her administration continued. She says they had to overcome planning and bureaucratic issues, including land-use changes and changes in the beneficiary authority.

### Former slaughterhouse / ex macello

A completed executive project to redevelop the former slaughterhouse near Piazzetta San Calogero as a civil protection and volunteer/emergency hub.

### Schools

She mentions several school-related projects:

- executive design work for Via delle Chiuse school;
- funding attempts for the Via Imera kindergarten;
- external works at the kindergarten;
- a feasibility project for the middle school gym;
- around €150,000 for the external area of the elementary school.

### Ceramic museum and tourism

Through the SNAI internal areas strategy, she says earlier funds of about €760,000 were confirmed for the ceramic museum and former kilns. Her administration continued this path, updated the project, obtained necessary opinions, and expects decrees to allow the work to proceed.

She also mentions slow mobility, tourism routes, a bike station, electric bike charging, and tourism-related improvements.

### Cemetery

She says the administration continued cemetery work, including around 120 new burial niches, and plans further transfers and exhumations to ensure dignified burial.

### Public lighting

She says the administration completed public lighting efficiency work, reducing costs and improving lighting in areas such as the historic center, castle area, Borgo Gammisini, Contrada Croce, and Contrada Gatto.

### Palazzo Fatta

She says a project was prepared for part of Palazzo Fatta to become a multifunctional room and tourist information office.

### Smaller projects

She briefly mentions a sensory garden, playgrounds, cultural activities, and other funding lines.

## Cultural identity and events

Near the end, Cascio defends spending time and energy on cultural events and identity projects. She says governing is not only about repairing roads and water networks, but also about community identity and economic/social life.

She specifically defends:

- the Targa Florio, saying Collesano was brought back into the event in 2025 and became more central in 2026;
- Carnival;
- the ceramic tradition and Collesano's recognition as a city of ceramics;
- participation in ceramic networks and events, including New York, Milan Design Week / Salone del Mobile, and the Sicilian Regional Assembly;
- the feast of the Madonna;
- local festivals and events that help businesses and community life.

## Final message of the speech

Cascio closes by saying her administration is not perfect and does not claim to be the best. She asks citizens to judge them by the work done, the projects completed, and the projects underway.

She says they are governing not an abstract municipality, but the town where they live, where their families live, and where their children go to school. She promises to continue working for Collesano with responsibility, humility, seriousness, determination, and passion.

## Very condensed version

In plain English: Tiziana Cascio uses the speech to defend her administration against Giovanni Meli's accusations. She rejects claims of intimidation, incompetence, lost funding, and lack of projects. She admits the PRG update was not completed on time but says they immediately began the PUG process. She argues the Casa di Comunità was not lost because of municipal negligence, but because the original building was technically unsuitable, and says alternative health services have been secured. She says the wastewater plant project was not fully ready before her administration and that no actual financing had been lost. She then lists many projects in roads, water, schools, public lighting, health, social services, sports, tourism, ceramics, and public buildings to show that her administration has been active and not merely living off the previous administration's work.
