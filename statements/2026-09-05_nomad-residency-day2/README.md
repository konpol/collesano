## Summary — speaker by speaker

**Marco Traina — coliving and "temporary citizens" `29:18–53:24`.** His Palermo coliving skips Booking/Airbnb entirely (~19-day average stays, direct bookings): the differentiator is community — "within a connected ecosystem an economy is generated." His thesis: turn tourists who *consume* the city (€4 arancine, historic centre without pharmacies, graduates fleeing to Milan) into **temporary citizens** on medium-long stays. From this came **Italianomad Fest** (March 8–15, with Comune di Palermo, AMAT, Confcommercio): ~€60k invested generating ~€500k across the city, 20-day average stays. Sharp point in Q&A: the missing ingredient is a *culture of circularity* — schools train competition, small Sicilian contexts breed envy; public administration should fund this but private actors carry it.

**Ilaria Foresti — community building `54:22–1:21:05`.** Ex-Milan career, two years in a camper van, now Palermo; helped build **Marketers**, Italy's largest digital-entrepreneurship community (20,000+ at offline events). Framework: community ≠ audience (many-to-many vs one-to-many, citing Seth Godin's *Tribes*); four ingredients — common purpose, the right platform (WhatsApp/Discord/Circle, *not* Instagram), shared values, a real offer. When *not* to build one: unstable brand, no time, no strategy. Core line: "relationships are the strongest positioning" in an AI-shaken marketing world.

**Ignacio "Nacho" Correia — AI-first organizations `1:21:52–1:47:02`.** Using AI ≠ being AI-first (the mobile-first analogy: banks added apps; Uber was *born* mobile). Three pillars: **Work** — outcome-first agent orchestration (he runs ~100 agents/month, ~8B tokens, zero employees); **Decisions** — AI prepares evidence and 3–4 options, humans only choose; **Knowledge** — agents self-document into playbooks ("the loop"), so knowledge compounds. Human skills that remain: delegation, judgment, systems thinking, taste. Costs from Q&A: ~$1k/month solo vs ~$25k/month for his corporate clients.

**Startup panel — "Sicily as a widespread accelerator" `1:54:32–2:55:50`.** PwC's Vincenzo Tarania [?]: periphery no longer exists — Oulu (Nokia, 6G) and Braga (Bosch, University of Minho) prove it; Sicily lacks not talent but *a strategy and a generational pact* — "talents must leave and find something to return to." Chiara Crisci (**Isola Catania**): bottom-up social-innovation hub model at Palazzo Biscari. Alessandro Dalcol [?] (**FabCube**): startups die of homogeneous technical teams, products nobody asked for, and squandered time. **Leonardo Gennaro [?] (BCC Madonie president):** the 121-year-old cooperative bank (profit €1.2M→€6M) sets aside credit-scoring algorithms for startups — evaluating people one-to-one with state guarantees; 270-member youth group with training in self-employment and AI. Structural problem: only 30% of €600M deposits can become local lending. **Fiasconaro is cited as the model's proof.** KORE's Vincenzo Fasone: "our city is Sicily" — drop the Palermo–Catania rivalry; the region is the minimum viable scale. Gerlando Maggio: AI water-leak detection (half-second detection, internet-free mesh) — and 3-year tender timelines that made him skip incorporating a startup at all.

**Pegah Moshir Pour with Sebastiana Cutugno — "Who has the right to choose where to live?" `3:01:27–3:58:58`.** The festival's emotional core, built on keywords and photos. *Home*: from her last school day in Tehran to her first in Potenza at age nine; "home is the people who love you"; Italian became her language of emotions. *Journey*: her parents' choice so their daughter could grow up free of propaganda; being a "third culture" — and discovering she lacked Italian citizenship. *Key*: Iran as a stratified society, the 1979 revolution "stolen" by extremism, no independent journalism (Cecilia Sala's detention cited), 90-day internet blackouts, protests since 1979 culminating in **Mahsa Jina Amini** (2022) and the Kurdish chant *Jin, Jiyan, Azadî*. She recounts "re-education sessions" her own parents endured, the Shiraz kiss as symbolic disobedience, a rape survivor's public testimony, a photographer's 20-year sentence, the persecuted Bahá'í. On *hate*: a fuel that must be directed — in Iran it walks with hope. Closing arc: awakening → hope → dream → future, with the Ventotene Manifesto invoked. In Q&A, the festival's question turned personal: asked about the right to choose where to live, she answers that returning to Iran would mean the gallows — and calls for a **global digital manifesto** against digital autocracies (Iran, China, Russia) and for EU institutions as citizens' voice on AI and data.

**Max Corona — *Storie di Brand… Siciliani* live `4:06:26–4:55:13`.** Theatrical storytelling: two lost Italians in Manhattan find Sicily in shop windows — a triangular **Balistreri** anchovy tin from Aspra (five generations from fisherman "U Zu Battista" to the interactive **Museo dell'Acciuga**), a **Fiasconaro** panettone from Castelbuono (gelateria 1953 → manna-based Sicilian panettone → White House, three popes, Soho store), and an "**Etna**" wine (the Gambino winery making "impossible" wine at 800 m — 46,000 visitors in 2025). The tourists turn out to be **Tony and Valeria on their 2017 honeymoon** — the origin story of the community that became Empiria and, ultimately, this festival. Finale with Michelangelo Balistreri of the Anchovy Museum: the "anchovy guitar," a group performance of Modugno's swordfish song, and his Sicilian poem against mafia stereotypes — *"Sicily is a poem; those ugly things belong neither to the people nor to this land of mine."* His line of the night: "They had nothing and had everything; we have everything and have nothing."
